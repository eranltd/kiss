One Tap is a signed note from Google that your server must check.

You open a site for the first time, and a small card says Continue as Ana. You tap once, and you are signed in, with no form and no password.

  1. Youactive session
  2. Your browserFedCM
  3. GoogleIdP
  4. Signed notecredential
  5. Your serververifyIdToken

Circled: your server, because the card and the note prove nothing until it checks Google's signature. The drawings below zoom into each part.

In plain words. Google already knows you are signed in to it in this browser. When a site asks, the browser shows a card. Your tap tells Google to write a note for that site, "this is Ana", and to sign it. The site's server checks the signature, then signs you in.

  1. The card shows up only when Google already knows you.

    In plain words. The site asks, and the browser asks Google who is signed in. If nobody is, nothing shows. If you are, the browser draws the card itself.

    Under the hood. (1) The page loads gsi/client and calls google.accounts.id.prompt(), and the browser asks Google for your signed-in accounts. (2) Google answers only from an active session; with several sessions you get an account chooser. (3) On Chrome 117 and later the browser owns the card (FedCM), shows the site's top-level domain, and needs no third-party cookies; the old use_fedcm_for_prompt switch is now ignored.

    // after https://accounts.google.com/gsi/client loads
google.accounts.id.initialize({
  client_id: "YOUR_CLIENT_ID",
  callback: (response) => sendToServer(response.credential),
});
google.accounts.id.prompt();

  2. One tap turns into a note that Google signs.

    In plain words. Your tap is your yes. Google writes a short note for this one site, "this is Ana", and signs it. The browser hands the note to the page.

    Under the hood. (1) The tap is consent; a first-time user sees a consent step first. (2) The browser passes your choice to Google for this site's client_id. (3) Google returns an ID token, a base64-encoded JWT it signed, in response.credential, and response.select_by says how you got there: fedcm for a tap, fedcm_auto for automatic sign-in.

  3. The note names you by an ID, not by your email.

    In plain words. The note says who wrote it, who it is for, and who you are. Key the user on the account ID (sub), not on the email.

    Under the hood. The token is a JWT: a header, the claims, and Google's signature over both. The claims include iss, aud, sub, email, email_verified, name, picture and exp. Store sub with the user and treat email as contact data.

  4. Your server checks the signature before it trusts the name.

    In plain words. The page sends the note to your server. Your server checks Google's signature and who the note is for. Only then does it sign you in, with its own cookie.

    Under the hood. (1) The page posts credential to your server; in redirect mode, also match the g_csrf_token cookie against the same field in the body. (2–3) A library call such as verifyIdToken() checks the signature with Google's public keys, re-fetched only when Cache-Control says so, and checks that aud is your client ID, iss is accounts.google.com or https://accounts.google.com, and exp has not passed. (4) Then find or create the user by sub and set your own session cookie.

    import { OAuth2Client } from "google-auth-library";
const client = new OAuth2Client();

const ticket = await client.verifyIdToken({
  idToken: req.body.credential,
  audience: CLIENT_ID,
});
const userId = ticket.getPayload().sub;

Close the card once, and it stops showing up.

Without FedCM, each close hides it longer: two hours, then one day, one week and four weeks, until a successful sign-in resets the count. With FedCM the browser sets its own cooldown and no longer tells the page why the card is missing (getNotDisplayedReason() is gone); in Chrome, reset it from the lock icon with Reset Permission.

Push further

What happens when a user signs out of my site?

With auto_select on, the next page with One Tap signs them straight back in. Call google.accounts.id.disableAutoSelect() on sign-out; it blocks automatic sign-in for one day and does not sign them out of Google.

Does it work in Safari and Firefox?

Not as the standard card. In Safari, Firefox and Chrome on iOS (Google calls them ITP browsers), itp_support: true shows a welcome page that opens a pop-up, and automatic sign-in is not supported.

Can I call Google APIs with this note?

No. Sign-in returns only ID tokens. An access token comes from Google's separate authorization API, which you call later.
